The problem

Payroll is the most reliable thing your business does. And the least useful.

Every month, on time, without fail — and at the end of it you have a bank confirmation, a spreadsheet, and nothing anyone outside your company can check.

01

One bad row stops everyone.

A wrong account number, a closed account, a new joiner who isn't set up yet — and the whole run bounces. Everybody waits, and finance spends the day on the phone.

02

The record lives in someone's inbox.

Payslips in one folder, transfer confirmations in another, corrections agreed over WhatsApp. When someone asks what was actually paid in March, answering it is an afternoon's work.

03

Your best track record counts for nothing.

Two years of paying eighty people on time is the clearest evidence your business is sound. It just isn't evidence anyone will accept, because nothing stops the numbers being edited after the fact.

The difference

One bad row shouldn't cost everyone their payday.

Payroll goes out as a single batch of hundreds of payments. In most systems, if one row is bad, the whole batch fails and nobody gets paid that day. Here, that one payment is held back — and every other payment in the run still goes through.

  • Only the affected payment stops. The rest of the run completes on schedule, untouched.
  • Somebody has to look at it. An administrator approves or rejects the held payment — and rejecting it requires a written reason.
  • The review joins the record too. Who decided, when, and why is kept permanently — so the decision itself can be checked later.

The longer game

Your payroll history is an asset. Today it evaporates.

A lender deciding whether to finance your business wants to know one thing: does money reliably go out on time? You already have that answer — twelve, twenty-four, thirty-six months of it. What you don't have is a version anyone will trust, because a record your own company can edit isn't evidence.

Payramid builds that record as a by-product of doing payroll. Automate payroll now; use the verified history to help eligible businesses reach regulated financing later.

Why now

Egypt first — on purpose.

A large base of small employers

Most employers in the country are small and mid-sized businesses, running payroll by hand, month after month, with no system underneath it.

A national push away from cash

Moving payments out of cash is national policy — and payroll is one of the largest recurring cash flows a business has.

A regulator that publishes a path

There is a published fintech sandbox, which means an emerging model has somewhere to be tested rather than merely argued about.

Live today

The platform runs. Payroll runs are uploaded, previewed, approved and permanently recorded. Employees have accounts and can see their own payment history. This is working software, not a mockup.

Not yet

We do not move the money. Payramid records that a payment was approved and owed; the transfer itself still happens on your existing rail. Moving money requires a payment licence, and our route to it is a licensed partner rather than becoming a payment institution ourselves.
